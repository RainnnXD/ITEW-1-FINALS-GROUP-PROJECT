A Weekend in Baguio

October 4, 2026

The morning fog rolled through the mountains as we started our walk around the city. The air was cool and carried the scent of pine trees — a reminder that we were somewhere truly different from the lowlands. Baguio greets you the moment you step off the bus: that first breath of cold mountain air, the sight of green hills disappearing into low clouds, and the sound of the city waking up at an unhurried highland pace.

We spent the first morning at Burnham Park — rowboats on the lagoon, the enormous old trees casting long shadows over the stone paths. The park was quiet, full of joggers and elderly couples doing their morning routines. There is something about being on the water, even a small lagoon, that slows a person down.

The Baguio Public Market was next — great heaps of strawberries, bundles of local vegetables, jars of ube halaya, and peanut brittle from a stall that had been in one family since the 1980s. We bought more than we could carry. In the craft section, Cordilleran woven bags and wooden carvings lined the stalls, each piece carrying the fingerprints of the highlands.

By afternoon we were at Mines View Lookout — the valley dropping away below us, layered with terraced farms and pine-covered ridges. We stood there longer than planned. There is something in that view that makes conversation unnecessary.
