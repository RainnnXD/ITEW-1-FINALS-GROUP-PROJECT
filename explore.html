Explore the Philippines

An overview of Luzon, Visayas, and Mindanao.

Luzon Visayas Mindanao At a glance
NORTH
Northern island group

Luzon

Luzon is the largest island group in the Philippines and home to the country’s capital, Manila. It offers a mix of busy cities, beautiful mountains, historic towns, and relaxing beaches. Whether you enjoy exploring nature, learning about Philippine history, or trying local food, Luzon has plenty of places worth visiting.

For nature lovers, Banaue in Ifugao is famous for its stunning rice terraces carved into the mountains. Mayon Volcano in Albay is another must-see destination, known for its nearly perfect cone shape. If you prefer cool weather and mountain views, Baguio City and Sagada are great places to explore. You can also visit La Union and Baler for surfing, or head to Batangas to enjoy its beaches and the scenic views of Taal Volcano.

Luzon is also rich in history and culture. In Vigan, Ilocos Sur, you can walk along cobblestone streets lined with old Spanish-style houses and experience a glimpse of the Philippines’ past. In Manila, you can visit Intramuros, a historic walled city filled with old churches, museums, and colonial landmarks. Each destination offers something different, making Luzon a great choice for travelers who want to experience both the traditional and modern sides of the Philippines.

  • Known for: Banaue Rice Terraces, Vigan’s heritage streets, Mayon Volcano, Taal Volcano, Baguio City, Sagada, La Union’s surfing spots, and Intramuros in Manila.
  • Best time: November to April

CENTRAL
Central island group

Visayas

The Visayas is the central island group of the Philippines, famous for its beautiful beaches, clear blue waters, colorful festivals, and welcoming communities. It is made up of several islands, including Cebu, Bohol, Negros, Panay, Samar, and Leyte. With its mix of natural wonders, historical landmarks, and exciting local experiences, the Visayas is a great destination for travelers who want to enjoy the beauty and culture of the Philippines.

For beach lovers, Boracay in Aklan is one of the most popular destinations, known for its white sand and stunning sunsets. Cebu offers exciting adventures such as swimming with whale sharks in Oslob, exploring Kawasan Falls, and visiting historic landmarks in Cebu City. In Bohol, travelers can admire the famous Chocolate Hills, see the tiny Philippine tarsier, and relax on the beaches of Panglao Island. Meanwhile, Iloilo is known for its heritage churches, delicious local food, and the lively Dinagyang Festival.

The Visayas is also known for its rich traditions and festivals. Events such as Sinulog in Cebu, Ati-Atihan in Aklan, and MassKara in Bacolod bring communities together through music, dancing, colorful costumes, and street celebrations. These festivals offer visitors a closer look at the region’s history, creativity, and strong sense of community.

  • Known for: Boracay’s white-sand beaches, Cebu’s waterfalls and historical sites, Bohol’s Chocolate Hills, Panglao Island, Iloilo’s heritage attractions, and the region’s colorful festivals.
  • Best time: November to May

SOUTH
Southern island group

Mindanao

Mindanao is the second-largest island group in the Philippines, known for its impressive mountains, beautiful coastlines, rich natural resources, and diverse cultures. Located in the southern part of the country, it offers a different kind of travel experience, with destinations ranging from peaceful islands and waterfalls to mountain landscapes and lively cities. Mindanao is also home to many Indigenous communities and Muslim Filipino communities whose traditions, crafts, food, and festivals contribute to the region’s cultural richness.

Nature lovers can visit Mount Apo, the highest mountain in the Philippines, located between Davao del Sur and North Cotabato. The island of Siargao is famous for its surfing spots, especially Cloud 9, as well as its lagoons, rock pools, and palm-lined roads. In Surigao del Sur, the Hinatuan Enchanted River attracts visitors with its striking blue water, while Camiguin, known as the “Island Born of Fire,” offers waterfalls, hot springs, volcanic landscapes, and scenic beaches.

Mindanao also has lively cities and unique cultural attractions. Davao City is known for its durian, nature parks, and access to Mount Apo. Cagayan de Oro attracts adventure seekers with activities such as whitewater rafting along the Cagayan de Oro River. Meanwhile, Zamboanga City is known for its colorful Vinta boats, coastal scenery, and Chavacano culture, which reflects a blend of local and Spanish influences.

Beyond its natural attractions, Mindanao has a rich cultural identity shaped by its many communities. Visitors can discover traditional textiles, local crafts, regional dishes, and cultural celebrations that reflect the island’s diverse heritage. Respecting local customs and learning about the communities you visit can make your trip more meaningful.

  • Known for: Siargao surfing, Camiguin, Mount Apo, Kadayawan Festival
  • Main gateways: Davao, Cagayan de Oro, and Siargao
  • Best time: Dry for much of the year. Siargao surf peaks September to November.
