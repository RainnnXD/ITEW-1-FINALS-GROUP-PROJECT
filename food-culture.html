Food & Culture

Taste the flavors. Meet the traditions. Every island has its own story on the plate.

See must-try dishes

Must-try Filipino dishes

A few classics to look for, plus where each one shines.

Everywhere

Adobo

Meat braised in vinegar, soy sauce, garlic, and pepper. The unofficial national dish.

Visayas

Cebu Lechon

Spit-roasted pig with crackling skin, stuffed with lemongrass and spices.

Everywhere

Halo-Halo

Shaved ice layered with sweet beans, fruit, leche flan, and ube ice cream.

Luzon

Sisig

Chopped, sizzling pork with onions and chili, born in Pampanga.

Mindanao

Kinilaw

Fresh fish cured in vinegar or citrus with ginger and chili. Best eaten by the coast.

Everywhere

Sinigang

A sour tamarind soup with pork, shrimp, or fish and plenty of vegetables.

Festivals & traditions

Music, color, and community. Dates shift each year, so check before you book.

January

Sinulog

Cebu City, Visayas

A grand street parade honoring the Santo Niño.

January

Ati-Atihan

Kalibo, Aklan

Face paint, drums, and tribal costumes fill the streets.

October

MassKara

Bacolod, Negros

Smiling masks and street dancing in the City of Smiles.

August

Kadayawan

Davao City, Mindanao

A thanksgiving for harvest, flowers, and Davao's cultures.

Eat and travel like a local

A little know-how goes a long way. Filipinos are known for warm hospitality, and small gestures are always appreciated.

Meals are often shared. If you are invited to eat, say yes. Hosts will often insist you take seconds.

Most meals use a spoon and fork. The fork pushes food onto the spoon. Kamayan (eating by hand) is common at celebrations.

Greet elders with "po" and "opo". Ask before photographing people, and dress modestly when visiting churches and mosques.
